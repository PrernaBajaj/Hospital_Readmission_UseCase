# Recommendation for the care-management lead

Yes, use the list as a ranking tool: each week call the top 5% of discharged patients (about 30 of 600), because in testing about 55 of every 100 people it picked came back within 30 days, compared with about 16 of every 100 patients overall. A score such as "46 in 100" can be read as an honest chance (in testing, patients scored around 46 in 100 came back about 49 in 100), but it is an estimate for people with a similar profile, not a prediction for that individual. Even a good list reaches only about one in six readmissions, so keep your usual judgment and referrals for patients who are not on it. We do not yet know that a call prevents a readmission, so for the first 8 to 12 weeks follow a random sample of patients ranked just below the cut-off without calling them and compare outcomes before changing staffing. Each week check that the four-week average share of listed patients who come back stays near 55%, and ask quality and clinical informatics to review before relying on the list if it falls below about 45% or if how a field is recorded at any site changes.
